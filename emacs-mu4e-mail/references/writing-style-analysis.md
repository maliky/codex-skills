# Writing Style from Historical Mail

Use when the user asks to study their own writing or adapt a draft to it. This is a corpus-reading workflow, not a personality assessment or an implementation plan for a communications application.

1. Inventory known current and historical sender aliases within the authorized mail corpus. Verify exact raw From headers; an address mentioned in a body or recipient field does not establish authorship.
2. Separate search hits, deduplicated messages, messages with authored prose, and texts actually read. Record queries, date coverage, excluded mailboxes, selection criteria, and gaps.
3. Remove quoted replies, forwarded text, signatures, boilerplate, and automated messages from style evidence. Preserve originals and do not discard distinct content merely because Message-IDs match.
4. For qualitative reading, select varied periods, languages, recipients, registers, lengths, and counterexamples. Describe patterns and limits; do not estimate corpus-wide frequency from a hand-picked sample. For quantitative claims, define a reproducible sampling method before analysis.
5. Keep observations tied to examples and context. A difference between two periods may reflect recipients or topics rather than a change in voice. Do not infer personal relationships or psychological traits from communication counts.
6. Adapt the requested draft while preserving its facts, purpose, relationship, and explicit tone preferences. Historical style evidence does not authorize adding personal disclosures or copying unrelated private details.

Store working excerpts, aliases, real message identifiers, and identity maps locally outside shared Git artifacts. Use synthetic examples for reusable code/tests. Work on the separate `uncours` research project requires its current README and `.DEV.org`; an initial scaffold is not evidence of a working importer or an approved corpus protocol.
